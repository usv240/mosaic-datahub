Mosaic is a metadata-aware privacy remediation agent built on DataHub. Its job is to catch a risk ordinary column scanners miss: several harmless-looking facts can become identifying only after lineage brings them together.

Here is the core example. ZIP code arrives from support, birth date from membership, and gender from demographics. No field is direct P I I. DataHub tells Mosaic where each field came from, where the combined export travels, and which downstream assets inherit the exposure.

The landing page teaches that idea before asking a visitor to configure anything. Four presets cover research exports, marketing activation, health analytics, and a safe engineering control. A new evaluator can run the complete investigation without credentials or setup.

In the research case, Mosaic first discovers independent column-lineage paths. It then validates only aggregate group counts in an isolated environment. The result is minimum k equals one, one hundred percent of records below k five, three downstream assets exposed, and zero raw person-level rows returned.

That last number is an architectural constraint. An agent may propose a query, but deterministic policy rejects identifiers, joins, filters, extra statements, and non-aggregate projection. Organization-owned thresholds decide the verdict. The model proposes; policy disposes.

Mosaic does more than display a warning. Its reversible mitigation lab compares privacy improvement with retained utility. Then metadata-aware code generation creates a reviewable pull-request bundle: a dbt model, enforced typed contract, aggregate-only privacy test, policy snapshot, provenance manifest, and reviewer summary. Every artifact carries the DataHub U R N and tamper-evident digests.

DataHub is not a logo on this project. Its schema, fine-grained lineage, downstream graph, Graph Q L API, M C P tools, tags, structured properties, Documents, and incidents form the context, action, and memory layers. Mosaic also contributed an upstream change that was merged into DataHub.

We tested the reader against DataHub's official showcase ecommerce pack in a real quickstart catalog that Mosaic did not create. It inspected eleven fields, classified three across two families, found only one independently evidenced upstream dataset, and correctly refused to invent a convergence finding. That negative control is part of the product, not a hidden failure.

For production warehouses, the Snowflake adapter uses a scoped identity and a read-only, query-tagged verification path. Credentials are never bundled, and public receipts hash identity context rather than exposing it.

Mosaic is privacy risk reduction, not proof of anonymity or legal compliance. Production still needs policy owners, least-privilege DataHub and warehouse identities, S S O, R B A C, and human approval. Mosaic turns hidden graph context into evidence and code a data team can review before risk spreads.
